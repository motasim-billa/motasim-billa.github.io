Md Motasim Billa

MS student in Information Science at the University of Arkansas at Little Rock.

I am a master’s student in Information Science at the University of Arkansas at Little Rock. My studies are about something simple and important: how knowledge is made, organized, and shared — how what one person learns finds its way to others. I also care about fairness in science. The people who do the work should get the credit they deserve, and people should be able to trust what they read.

My story began in Dhaka, Bangladesh, among books and libraries. Later, I worked as a journalist. Every day I asked questions, and I learned to check what is true before I wrote it down. Then came a fellowship year in America, and it opened a whole new world to me. Step by step, that path brought me to information science, and to Arkansas.

I love travelling and seeing new places. I love exploring different fields of study, and new technology always makes me curious. I enjoy reading, and I enjoy a good debate. My road has taken me through libraries, newsrooms, and classrooms in two countries — and I am still curious about where it leads next.

The Invisible Infrastructure of Biomedical Research: An Exploratory Analysis of Software Mentions and Sentence-Level Citation MarkingJournal articleUnder review

M. M. Billa (sole author) · Scientometrics · 2026

Modern biomedical papers run on software that is usually mentioned in passing rather than cited. This study maps those software mentions across the literature and examines sentence-level citation marking — where in a paper credit is given, and what that placement says about how scholarly credit actually flows.

Livelock in the Agent Harness: Delegation-Induced Liveness Failure in LLM SystemsConference paperAccepted

M. Hasan, A. Khan, H. Rashid, M. M. Billa, S. Islam, and M. Milanova · Proceedings of the IEEE International Conference on Trust, Privacy and Security in Intelligent Systems and Applications (TPS) · 2026 · Application Track

Studies livelock in LLM agent harnesses — systems where agents keep delegating work to one another and nothing ever finishes — and names and characterizes this delegation-induced liveness failure. To appear; one of 16 papers accepted from 146 submissions.

In progress

A Position-Based Citation-Credit Model for Multi-Authored PublicationsMS thesis

M. M. Billa · University of Arkansas at Little Rock · Advisor: Daniel Berleant, Ph.D.

A citation is a single unit of credit, but a paper can have two authors or two hundred — and where a name sits in the byline carries information flat counting throws away. This thesis develops a position-sensitive model for allocating that credit among co-authors, and tests how different attribution choices change researcher-level bibliometric assessment.

My newspaper articles

Before graduate school I reported from Dhaka University for the Bengali daily Shomoyer Alo (byline: Motasim Billa Naim), covering the campus in 2019 — admission reform, student movements, and the small stories in between. From 2023 to 2024 I was a data journalist at Bangladesh Post in Dhaka, where I wrote more than 200 data-informed and analytical articles: turning datasets, budgets, and official statistics into evidence-based, public-interest reporting that readers could check for themselves. That newsroom training — ask what the numbers actually say, and show your working — is the same instinct I now bring to scientometrics.

Like most newspaper stories, the printed pieces below ran in two parts: a first part under the headline, and the rest continued — “jumped” — to an inside page. Each card below shows only the titled first part, complete and uncropped. Click a clipping to read it enlarged — and where the continuation page survives among my scans, it appears there, after the first part, exactly as a reader of the paper would have turned to it. Where no continuation page is among my scans, the enlarged view says so rather than filling the gap.

These clippings are the pieces I kept from my reporting years, scanned from the printed pages. Together they are the whole of my surviving newspaper archive — every article and report in the collection is on this page, each with its continuation where one survives among the scans.

The CCI year — the best experience of my life

2021 – 2022 · Virginia, USA · U.S. Department of State CCI Program

In 2021 I was selected for the Community College Initiative (CCI) Program, a U.S. Department of State fellowship that brings participants from around the world to spend an academic year at an American community college. I still call it, without hesitation, one of the best experiences of my life. My placement was Northern Virginia Community College, where I studied Business Management and Leadership Development, completed an Associate’s degree (GPA 3.42/4.00), and earned a place on the Dean’s Honor List in Spring 2022.

But the degree was only part of what CCI is designed to give. The program deliberately builds a year out of more than coursework — internships, community service, and cultural exchange are woven through it — and it was those parts that stayed with me most. I volunteered as a Book Buddy with Alexandria City Public Schools, sitting with young readers one-to-one each week and watching hesitant kids turn into confident ones. I lived the ordinary rhythms of Northern Virginia: classes, work, service, and the small daily negotiations of a new country. For a student from Bangladesh, it was a full year of seeing how another society organizes its schools, its libraries, and its public life — from the inside.

That year changed the direction of mine. It reshaped how I think about education and public institutions, gave me friends and mentors across the world, and planted the idea that I would come back to the United States for graduate study. When I arrived at the University of Arkansas at Little Rock for my MS in Information Science, I was, in a real sense, continuing a journey that CCI started.

Leading BYI — the Bangladesh Youth Initiative

2020 – 2021 · University of Dhaka

Before the fellowship came a lesson in leading at home. At the University of Dhaka I served as president of the university unit of the Bangladesh Youth Initiative (BYI) in 2020–21 — a student community built around young people taking responsibility for something larger than their own degrees. The presidency was my first real experience of holding an organization together: setting a direction, persuading busy students to give their time to it, and answering for the result when plans met reality.

It taught me things no classroom had. Leadership, I learned, is mostly listening carefully and following through — doing the unglamorous work between meetings that makes the visible work possible. In the same season I coordinated Surjasen Bitarka Dhara, the debating circle of Surjasen Hall (2020), which sharpened the other half of the skill: building an argument, defending it in public, and losing one gracefully. Those Dhaka years — organizing, debating, reporting for the campus paper — are the foundation everything since has been built on.

From the newsroom to the library

2019 – 2024 · Dhaka

My working life began in print. In 2019 I was the Dhaka University correspondent for the Bengali daily Shomoyer Alo, covering admission reform, campus movements, and the small human stories in between — the clippings are on the Newspaper Writing page. After my degree I joined Bangladesh Post as a data journalist (2023–24) and wrote more than 200 data-informed, analytical articles, turning budgets and official statistics into reporting readers could check for themselves.

Alongside the newsroom ran the library and the lab: an internship at Dhaka University Library in 2023, working through cataloging, classification, and user services at Bangladesh’s largest academic library; and a data science internship at Somikoron AI (2022–23), building an NLP-based recommendation system. Journalism taught me to interrogate data; the library taught me how knowledge is organized and kept; the internship taught me to compute with text. Information science is where those three threads join — and at UALR, as a graduate assistant in JMC supporting faculty teaching and research, I now work on the other side of the systems I once reported on.

Beyond the desk

Reading · Debating · Volunteering

This site is academic, but it is also mine — so here is some of what fills the hours when I am not counting citations.

Reading

Reading for pleasure is a habit I have argued for in print: my feature “Reading for pleasure boosts kids’ brain development” grew out of research I could not stop thinking about — and out of a childhood spent reading well past bedtime. The habit has survived graduate school; there is always a book on the desk that has nothing to do with the thesis.

Debating

At the University of Dhaka I coordinated Surjasen Bitarka Dhara, the debating circle of Surjasen Hall, in 2020, and served as president of the university unit of the Bangladesh Youth Initiative in 2020–21. Debating taught me to build an argument under time pressure — and, just as usefully, to lose one gracefully.

Tutoring & volunteering

During my exchange year in Virginia I volunteered as a Book Buddy with Alexandria City Public Schools (2021–22), reading one-to-one with young students each week. It remains some of the most rewarding time I have spent in the United States — and it circles back to the reading I still love.
